# Annotated bibliography

## Status

This is a seed bibliography from an exploratory search on 2026-08-13. It is not the final literature corpus. Citation counts are transient Consensus metadata recorded only to describe the discovery context; they are not quality scores.

## Design Science Research

### Peffers et al. (2007)

Peffers, K., Tuunanen, T., Rothenberger, M. A., and Chatterjee, S. (2007). “A Design Science Research Methodology for Information Systems Research.” *Journal of Management Information Systems*, 24, 45–77. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2753/MIS0742-1222240302). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/a-design-science-research-methodology-for-information-peffers-tuunanen/d0d5486d4f1b56fc8ffbc0961cc08774/?utm_source=chatgpt).

- **Discovery metadata:** 7,401 citations in Consensus at retrieval.
- **Contribution:** Provides the six-stage DSRM process: problem identification and motivation, objectives, design and development, demonstration, evaluation and communication.
- **Use in this study:** Candidate backbone for structuring the thesis and distinguishing artifact construction from demonstration and evaluation.
- **Limitation:** Does not determine this study’s artifact boundary, empirical strategy or evaluation criteria.

### Hevner et al. (2004)

Hevner, A. R., March, S. T., Park, J., and Ram, S. (2004). “Design Science in Information Systems Research.” *MIS Quarterly*, 28, 75–105. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/25148625). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/design-science-in-information-systems-research-hevner-march/ec841b60e152590097388cac52c8ca92/?utm_source=chatgpt).

- **Discovery metadata:** 13,042 citations in Consensus at retrieval.
- **Contribution:** Frames design science as knowledge generation through building and applying artifacts, with attention to rigor, relevance and evaluation.
- **Use in this study:** Supports the argument that the YAML profile must be both practically relevant and connected to a defensible knowledge base.
- **Limitation:** High-level guidelines still require operationalization for a 15 ECTS study.

### Venable et al. (2016)

Venable, J., Pries-Heje, J., and Baskerville, R. (2016). “FEDS: a Framework for Evaluation in Design Science Research.” *European Journal of Information Systems*, 25, 77–89. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1057/ejis.2014.36). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/feds-a-framework-for-evaluation-in-design-science-research-venable-pries-heje/62e9e81d258f5122a19eee0521478d38/?utm_source=chatgpt).

- **Discovery metadata:** 1,025 citations in Consensus at retrieval.
- **Contribution:** Structures evaluation by purpose, timing, paradigm and evaluated properties, distinguishing formative and summative as well as artificial and naturalistic evaluation.
- **Use in this study:** Supports a feasible combination of schema/conformance testing, scenario-based demonstration and structured expert review.
- **Limitation:** Evaluation design must be scaled to the thesis and must not imply validation of legal compliance.

## Data-governance automation

### Nadal et al. (2022)

Nadal, S., Jovanovic, P., Bilalli, B., and Romero, O. (2022). “Operationalizing and automating Data Governance.” *Journal of Big Data*, 9, article 117. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40537-022-00673-5). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/operationalizing-and-automating-data-governance-nadal-jovanovic/61eec95b985c5d318b289f215f1478f0/?utm_source=chatgpt).

- **Discovery metadata:** 31 citations in Consensus at retrieval.
- **Contribution:** Proposes metadata artifacts and executable processes for operationalizing and automating data governance in a zoned data-lake setting.
- **Use in this study:** Direct evidence that metadata artifacts can drive governance processes rather than merely document them.
- **Limitation:** The architectural and use-case context does not establish coverage of Swedish public-sector semantics, archival requirements or open data-product standards.

## Public-sector metadata and archival concerns

### Bountouri et al. (2009)

Bountouri, L., Papatheodorou, C., Soulikias, V., and Stratis, M. (2009). “Metadata interoperability in public sector information.” *Journal of Information Science*, 35, 204–231. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0165551508098601). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/metadata-interoperability-in-public-sector-information-bountouri-papatheodorou/ada355b0cde95579a1da458bcf07c1d2/?utm_source=chatgpt).

- **Discovery metadata:** 29 citations in Consensus at retrieval.
- **Contribution:** Compares application-profile and ontology-based approaches to metadata interoperability for public-sector information.
- **Use in this study:** Supports treating a standards profile as a legitimate interoperability design approach rather than creating an isolated schema.
- **Limitation:** Predates current DCAT-AP-SE, ODCS and ODPS versions and does not address Swedish archival governance.

### Chorley (2017)

Chorley, K. M. (2017). “The challenges presented to records management by open government data in the public sector in England: a case study.” *Records Management Journal*, 27, 149–158. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/RMJ-09-2016-0034). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/the-challenges-presented-to-records-management-by-open-chorley/d6bfbe8f508b53afa3bd506a0da0455e/?utm_source=chatgpt).

- **Discovery metadata:** 24 citations in Consensus at retrieval.
- **Contribution:** Identifies interconnected records-management challenges associated with public-sector open-data practice, including accuracy and integrity concerns.
- **Use in this study:** Supports the claim that publication and reuse of data cannot be cleanly separated from records-management responsibilities.
- **Limitation:** Small, England-specific case study; it cannot establish Swedish legal or institutional requirements.

## Initial synthesis

The seed literature supports three parts of the problem:

1. DSR provides a defensible method for designing and evaluating a machine-readable governance artifact.
2. Metadata can operationalize and automate governance processes.
3. Public-sector interoperability and open-data practice have records-management implications.

The specifically proposed gap remains **provisional**: the reviewed seed papers do not yet show an evaluated, open-standard profile that integrates Swedish public-sector semantic, accountability and archival-readiness requirements into machine-readable data-product governance. A systematic search and authoritative-source mapping are required before this can be claimed as the thesis research gap.
